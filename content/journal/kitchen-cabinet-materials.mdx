When you compare kitchen quotes, the biggest differences are usually hidden in the materials. Two kitchens can look similar in a drawing and be very different after five years of daily use.

Understanding the main materials helps you decide where to spend and where to save. This guide covers four areas: the cabinet board, the door finish, the counter top and the hardware.

## Cabinet boards

The board is the structure of the cabinet, the part you do not see from the front. It carries the weight of pots, plates and appliances, so it matters more than its hidden position suggests.

Common options include:

- **Plywood:** layers of wood glued together. It holds screws well and is generally strong, but quality varies widely between grades.
- **MDF:** a smooth, dense fibreboard. It is good for painted and shaped doors, but standard grades can swell if water gets in.
- **Particle board:** the most economical option. It suits dry areas and light loads, but is the most sensitive to moisture.
- **Moisture-resistant (MR) and water-resistant grades:** versions of the above made to handle humidity. They are worth considering for the sink and hob areas.

Whatever you choose, ask how the edges are sealed. Water usually enters through unsealed edges, so good edge banding matters as much as the board itself.

## Door finishes

Doors are what you see and touch, so they set the look of the kitchen and its day-to-day maintenance.

- **Laminate:** durable, easy to clean, and available in many colours and wood-look patterns.
- **Acrylic or high-gloss panels:** reflective and modern, but they show fingerprints and scratches more easily.
- **Lacquer or paint:** smooth, rich colour. It can chip and needs careful care.
- **Veneer:** thin real-wood layer, offering natural grain. It can be sensitive to sun and moisture.
- **Solid wood:** warm and long-lasting, but heavy, and affected by humidity changes.

Think about your cooking. In a kitchen with heavy frying, a finish that wipes clean quickly is a real benefit.

## Counter tops

Counters take heat, knives, spills and weight, so choose one that suits how you cook.

- **Granite and natural stone:** hard and heat-tolerant, with a natural look. Some stones need sealing.
- **Quartz (engineered stone):** consistent colour and patterns, low maintenance, but sensitive to very high heat.
- **Solid surface:** seamless joins and repairable, though it can mark with hot pans.
- **Laminate:** the most affordable option, with limited heat and moisture tolerance.

Ask for a physical sample, and try it under the lights in your own kitchen.

## Hardware

Hardware is a small part of the cost and a big part of the experience. Hinges, drawer runners and lift-up mechanisms are used dozens of times a day.

- **Hinges:** soft-close hinges slow the door as it shuts and reduce wear.
- **Drawer runners:** full-extension runners let you reach the back of a drawer, and good ones carry heavy loads smoothly.
- **Handles or handle-free fronts:** handle-free designs look clean but need the right channel or push-open mechanism.
- **Internal fittings:** pull-out bins, corner units and cutlery trays make storage easier to use.

If you are cutting costs, avoid cutting here. A good finish on poor hardware starts to feel cheap quickly.

## Matching materials to budget

You rarely need top-grade everything. A sensible approach is to spend on what takes the most wear, and keep the rest simple:

1. **Hardware and the board near the sink and hob.** These parts face moisture and daily use.
2. **The counter top.** It is touched constantly.
3. **Door finish.** Choose one that suits your cooking and cleaning habits.
4. **Decorative extras.** These are the easiest to add later, so they are the first to cut.

## Care and maintenance

Even the best materials need basic care.

- Wipe spills quickly, especially near the sink and hob.
- Use mild cleaners, and avoid abrasive pads on glossy or painted surfaces.
- Use trivets and boards on counter tops, even if the surface is heat-tolerant.
- Check hinges and runners occasionally, and tighten any that have worked loose.
- Keep the area under the sink dry, and fix leaks early.

Ask your supplier for written care instructions for each material, because they differ.

## Ask about warranties

Warranties tell you how confident a supplier is in their work. Ask what is covered, for how long, and who to call if something fails. Check whether the warranty covers hardware, board and finishes separately, and what voids it. Keep the paperwork safe.

## Common questions

**Is more expensive always better?** Not necessarily. A higher price can mean better board, hardware or finish, or it can mean a brand name. Ask what you are getting for the difference.

**Can I mix materials?** Yes. Many kitchens use a hard-wearing finish on base cabinets and a lighter one on wall cabinets, or add a wood-look section for warmth.

**How do I judge quality from a sample?** Check the edges, the weight, how the door closes, and how smoothly drawers run. Ask where each piece is made and what warranty it carries.

**Which parts are hardest to change later?** The carcass and the counter top. Doors and handles can be replaced more easily, so put your spending on what is hard to redo.
