A small bedroom does not have to feel cramped. Most of what makes a room feel small is not its size but how it is arranged: too much furniture, too little light, and storage that spills into the floor space.

The ideas below work for most small bedrooms, whether you are planning a new room or improving one you already have.

## Start with light and colour

Natural light is the biggest help. Keep the window as clear as possible, and avoid blocking it with tall furniture.

Light, soft colours on the walls reflect more light and make the edges of the room less noticeable. That does not mean everything has to be white. A mid-tone on one wall can add depth, as long as the ceiling and the other walls stay light.

Use the same colour family on walls, curtains and bedding. When there are fewer breaks between colours, the eye reads the room as one larger space.

Lighting matters at night too. A single ceiling light can flatten a room. Add bedside lamps or wall lights, so the corners are lit and the room feels bigger.

## Think carefully about bed placement

The bed is the largest object in the room, so where it goes decides everything else.

- **Against the longest wall** often leaves the most open floor on the other side.
- **In the centre of a wall** with a bedside table on each side looks balanced, but needs enough width.
- **In a corner or against the window wall** can free up floor space in tight rooms, but check that you can still make the bed comfortably.

Leave enough room to open wardrobe doors fully and to walk around without turning sideways. Sliding doors help when the clearance is tight.

## Use storage above and below

Floor space is limited, so use the vertical space.

- **Under the bed:** a bed with drawers or a lift-up base can hold bedding, off-season clothing and luggage.
- **Above the bed:** shelves or cabinets over the headboard work well if the ceiling is high enough and the shelves feel secure.
- **Tall storage:** a floor-to-ceiling wardrobe uses the full wall and avoids the dead space above a normal one.
- **Wall-mounted bedside tables:** these keep the floor visible, which makes the room feel larger.

## Mirrors and curtains

A large mirror opposite or beside the window reflects light and creates a sense of depth. A mirrored wardrobe door does the same job while doubling as storage.

Hang curtains high, close to the ceiling, and let them reach the floor. This draws the eye upward and makes the walls look taller. Choose light fabrics that let daylight in, and add a blackout layer if you need darkness for sleeping.

## Consider built-ins

In a small room, fitted furniture often makes better use of space than loose pieces. A built-in wardrobe can run wall to wall, fit around a window or work around awkward corners, and it removes the gaps where dust and clutter collect.

A designer can also plan a built-in headboard with shelves or lighting, or a small desk that folds away when not in use. These details are made to your exact measurements, which is hard to achieve with ready-made furniture.

## Make the most of awkward corners

Small rooms often have awkward features, such as a column, a sloping wall or a door in an inconvenient place. Instead of working around them, let them work for you.

- A **column** can host a narrow shelf or a mirror.
- A **recess** can become a built-in desk or a reading nook.
- A **short wall** can hold a compact dresser or a bench at the foot of the bed.
- A **door that swings into the room** can sometimes be replaced with a sliding or outward-opening one to free floor space.

## Choose furniture to scale

Furniture that is too big for the room makes it feel cramped, however well it is arranged. Measure the room, then measure each piece, and mark its footprint on the floor with tape before you buy. Look for slim bedside tables, a bed with a low profile, and pieces with exposed legs so you can see the floor beneath them. Seeing more floor makes the room read as larger.

## Common questions

**Should I paint a small bedroom dark?** It can work if the room has good light and you want a cosy feel. Dark colours make the walls less noticeable, but they need more lighting. Light colours are the safer choice for making a room feel larger.

**Do I need a bedside table?** Not always. A narrow shelf, a wall-mounted ledge or a lamp on a bracket can replace a table and keep the floor clear.

**How can I add storage without a bigger wardrobe?** Use the space under the bed, over the door and above the headboard, and add hooks or a rail on a free wall.

**Is a rug a good idea?** Yes, if it is sized so that it extends beyond the bed's sides. A very small rug can make the room look smaller.

## Keep it uncluttered

Even the best layout will feel small if surfaces are covered. Keep bedside tables to the essentials, give everything a home, and avoid adding extra furniture because there is a gap. Empty space is part of the design.
