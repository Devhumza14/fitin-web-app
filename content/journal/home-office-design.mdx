Many homes now need a place to work, whether that is full-time remote work, a few days a week, or a quiet spot for study and bills. The mistake people make most often is squeezing a desk into a leftover corner and then avoiding it.

A home office that gets used is comfortable, well lit and separated, mentally if not physically, from the rest of the home. The suggestions below will help you build one, even in a small space.

## Choose the spot first

Before you think about furniture, decide where the office goes.

- **A spare room** gives the most privacy and lets you close the door at the end of the day.
- **A bedroom corner** works if you have no spare room, but try to keep the desk out of sight of the bed.
- **A living area nook** suits shorter sessions, and a fitted desk can be designed to blend in.
- **A landing or hallway** can host a slim desk if you fit it to the width of the space.

Look for natural light, a quiet location and a power supply close by. If you take video calls, think about noise from the kitchen or street.

## Desk and chair basics

You will spend many hours here, so comfort is not optional.

- **Desk height:** your forearms should rest roughly level when you type, with shoulders relaxed.
- **Desk depth:** deep enough to keep a screen at a comfortable distance from your eyes.
- **Chair:** supportive, adjustable and suited to long sittings. This is the item worth spending on.
- **Feet:** they should rest flat on the floor, or on a footrest.
- **Legroom:** leave space under the desk so you can move freely.

If you are fitting a custom desk, share your height and the equipment you use with your designer so the dimensions suit you.

## Light and screens

Place your desk so daylight comes from the side rather than straight behind or in front of the screen, which causes glare. Use curtains or blinds that diffuse light rather than block it.

For evenings, add a task lamp that lights your work surface without shining into your eyes. Warm, even light is easier on the eyes than a single bright spot.

Cable management is part of lighting and layout too. Plan sockets and cable routes in advance, so wires stay out of sight and out of the way.

## Storage

Clutter is the quickest way to lose focus. Aim to keep the desktop clear and to give everything a home.

- **Drawers** for stationery, chargers and small items.
- **Wall shelves** for books and files you use often.
- **Closed cabinets** for paperwork you rarely need.
- **A pinboard or whiteboard** for the few things you need in view.

Fitted storage can be designed around your desk, so the area is tidy even when you are busy.

## Your video-call background

If you take calls from home, think about what appears behind you. A tidy shelf, a plain wall or a simple piece of art looks professional and keeps the focus on you. Avoid windows directly behind you, which turn you into a silhouette, and check how the room looks on screen before an important meeting.

## Think about sound

Noise is one of the biggest complaints about working from home. If you take calls, you need privacy, and the people around you need quiet.

- A **solid door** blocks far more sound than a hollow one.
- **Rugs, curtains and upholstered furniture** soften echo, so you sound clearer on calls.
- **Bookshelves** on a shared wall add a little sound insulation.
- **Position the desk** away from the kitchen and the front door if you can.

## Plan for the long term

A home office may change with your life. A desk that can be extended, shelves that can be moved and sockets that are easy to reach will cope with a new monitor or a second person. Fitted pieces are best planned with some spare capacity, since adding to them later is harder than allowing for it at the start.

## Make it feel separate

Even in a shared room, small signals help your mind switch into work mode. A dedicated lamp, a closed laptop at the end of the day, or a screen that slides over the desk can mark the start and end of working hours.

## Common questions

**How big does a home office need to be?** Big enough for a desk, a chair you can move in, and the storage you use. Many people work well in a compact nook that is well planned.

**Should I buy a standing desk?** Some people like the option of changing position during the day. Try one before you buy if you can, and make sure the model is stable at your height.

**Where should the printer and files go?** Keep them within reach but out of sight, in a cabinet or on a shelf beside the desk, so they do not crowd the work surface.

**Can the office double as a guest room?** Yes. A sofa bed or a fold-down bed with fitted storage lets one room do both jobs, but plan the layout so each use works properly.
