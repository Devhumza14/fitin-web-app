A bathroom is a small room that works hard. It deals with water every day, and its mistakes, such as poor waterproofing or weak ventilation, show up slowly and are expensive to fix.

Whether you want a quick refresh or a complete renovation, the same principles apply: get the hidden parts right first, then make the visible parts look good.

## Refresh or renovate?

Start by deciding how far you want to go.

**A refresh** leaves the layout and the main fittings in place. It might include new taps, a new mirror, fresh paint, better lighting, new accessories and a deep clean of the grout. It is quick and has little disruption.

**A renovation** changes the layout, the tiles or the plumbing. It takes longer and costs more, but it fixes problems a refresh cannot, such as leaks, damp, cracked tiles or a layout that does not work.

If you are seeing damp patches, peeling paint or loose tiles, a refresh will only hide the problem. Find the cause first.

## Vanity and storage

A vanity is often the centrepiece, and it solves a storage problem at the same time.

- **Choose depth and width carefully.** A vanity that sticks out too far makes a small bathroom feel tight.
- **Wall-hung units** keep the floor visible and make cleaning easier.
- **Drawers** are easier to use than shelves behind doors.
- **Moisture-resistant materials** are important for anything near water.
- **A double vanity** suits households where two people use the bathroom at the same time, if the room is wide enough.

Add a tall cabinet or recessed niches in the wall for towels and toiletries, so surfaces stay clear.

## Tiles and waterproofing

Waterproofing is the most important and least visible part of a bathroom. It sits behind the tiles, and when it fails, water finds its way into walls and neighbouring rooms.

- **Ask what waterproofing system is used** and which areas it covers, including floors, wet walls and corners.
- **Make sure it is tested** before tiles are laid.
- **Give the floor a gentle slope** towards the drain so water does not pool.
- **Choose floor tiles with grip.** Smooth, polished tiles are slippery when wet.
- **Pick grout that suits the room.** Lighter grout shows stains, and some types need sealing.

Large tiles mean fewer grout lines, which can make a small bathroom look bigger and easier to clean.

## Lighting and mirrors

Lighting changes how a bathroom feels. Plan layers:

- **Ceiling light** for general use.
- **Light at the mirror**, on both sides if possible, to avoid shadows on your face.
- **A softer light** for night-time use.

Use fittings rated for damp areas, and have a qualified electrician place them. A large mirror reflects light and makes the room feel more open. A mirror with built-in lighting or a shelf can also save space.

## Ventilation

Without good ventilation, moisture lingers and leads to mould, peeling paint and a damp smell. If the bathroom has a window, make sure it can open. If not, an extractor fan sized for the room is essential, ideally one that runs on a timer or a humidity switch. Raise this early, since ducting needs planning before ceilings are closed.

## Other details worth planning

- Hooks and towel rails placed where they are easy to reach.
- A niche or ledge inside the shower for bottles.
- A bathroom door that does not swing into the vanity or the toilet.
- Hot water supply and pressure, which affect how fixtures perform.

## Fixtures and fittings

The fittings you choose affect comfort every day.

- **Taps and mixers:** choose reliable, easy-to-clean designs with spare parts available locally.
- **Showers:** consider water pressure before choosing a showerhead, and a glass screen or curtain that keeps the floor dry.
- **Toilets:** wall-hung models save floor space and make cleaning easier but need a strong wall and hidden cistern.
- **Accessories:** hooks, rails and holders should be fixed securely and placed within easy reach.

## Plan the order of work

For a renovation, the order matters. Remove old fittings and tiles first, then complete plumbing and electrical work, then waterproofing, then tiling, then fixtures, and finally painting and accessories. Allow waterproofing and tile adhesive to cure before the next stage, and ask your contractor how long each step needs.

## Common questions

**Can I change the position of the toilet or shower?** Often yes, but it depends on where the drainage runs. Ask your designer to check before you fall in love with a layout.

**Is it worth changing only the tiles?** If the waterproofing underneath is sound, new tiles can transform a bathroom. If you have leaks or damp, address those first, since new tiles will not fix them.

**How do I make a small bathroom look larger?** Use light colours, large tiles, a big mirror, wall-hung fittings and good lighting. Keep surfaces clear.

**What can I do without a contractor?** Paint, replace accessories and mirrors, upgrade lighting where safe, and clean and re-grout. Leave plumbing and electrical work to qualified people.
